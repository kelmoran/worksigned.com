Trust & Verification

How the proof works

WorkSigned signs a daily record of a researcher’s work and pins it to a public, hash-chained log. Any independent verifier can audit the record directly, without relying on WorkSigned.

The idea: Each day, the work is folded into a single hash. WorkSigned signs that hash with the day’s date and the researcher’s identity, links it to the previous day’s hash, and writes it to a public, hash-chained log. One independent time-stamp issuer pins the whole log to a clock no party controls, once per day. The proof is the signed record + the actual work files — never the hash alone.

How it works

1
The researcher works
The researcher edits, saves, and runs work in the project the normal way. The platform records the day’s activity.
2
One hash of the day
The day’s files, file events, and user events are combined into a single snapshot. That snapshot gets one hash.
3
WorkSigned signs it
WorkSigned signs the hash together with the day’s date, the researcher’s identity, and a link to the previous day’s hash. This is the signed record.
4
It is written to the public log
The signed record is appended to a public, hash-chained log that any party can read and verify.
5
One time-stamp per day
Once a day, an independent time-stamp issuer stamps the log’s head. That single stamp pins every record that day to a clock outside every party’s control.
# Two consecutive days in the public, hash-chained log 2026-09-21 researcher / project snap=77b0a2…e04f size=3.9MB ← chain: 0000…0000 (genesis) • signed 2026-09-22 researcher / project snap=3fa9c1…c21b size=4.2MB ← chain: 77b0a2…e04f (previous day) • signed → each day links to the one before it; drop or add a day and the chain breaks

The layers

The proof is built in layers, each one adding a specific guarantee. Understanding which layer does the work is the whole point.

LayerWhat it isWhat it proves
Original data The day’s files, file events, user events, and optional biometrics — the actual work. This is the work itself. It remains with the researcher and the institution.
Snapshot archive A packaged copy of the original data. Required with the signed record. Without it, a verifier holds a hash and nothing behind it.
Snapshot hash + size One hash of the archive, and its size. On its own: nothing. Both values can be changed at will. The signature below is what makes it meaningful.
Signed record The snapshot hash + size, the day’s date, the researcher’s identity, and a link to the prior record — signed with WorkSigned’s private key. The researcher held this exact hash on that date, in this order. The archive re-hashes to it; change one byte and it no longer verifies.
Signed record + archive The signed record together with the actual work files. The researcher held this work on that date, and the archive is the work the record points to. The complete, checkable proof.

What it proves

The work existed
On that date, by that researcher
On date T, the researcher held this exact hash — bound to the researcher’s identity — and the work re-hashes to it. Change one byte and the record no longer verifies.
The sequence held
Nothing dropped or added
Each day’s record links to the previous one. A verifier can walk the whole history and confirm nothing was deleted or inserted. A timestamp alone cannot do this — the chain can.
Any party can check it
Trust is verifiable
The public can trust the record because WorkSigned signs it — and WorkSigned’s own signing log is hashed, chained, and signed, in turn by an independent time-stamp issuer.

What about AI?

WorkSigned does not attest that the work was unaided. But it records more than the finished file. It records the keystrokes, the optional biometrics, and the day-to-day evolution of the work as it is produced.

That leaves a trail a reviewer can read. Work that is pasted in all at once looks very different from work that is built up over hours and days, with edits, corrections, and drafts. The record makes that difference visible.

So the system gives strong, convincing evidence of how the work was made. It is not a definitive proof of human authorship. But it is the kind of record that can provide strong evidence of a human vs. AI origin of work. AI typing does exist, but to date no known system exists that can recreate the entire content-creation process action by action, including the intermediate steps, in a human-like way.

In a dispute

Stated plainly. WorkSigned settles the important question that matters between claimants: who held the work first. It does not claim the work was original or unaided — credit is a human claim, and the system proves priority: that a specific researcher committed a specific body of work to a verifiable, independently time-stamped record on a specific date, in an unbroken chain.

It also shows the path the data took. The record captures the external services and systems the work contacted during production — outbound calls, uploads, and the state of the work before and after each. In a dispute over access to sensitive work, this gives a dated, verifiable account of the supply chain: what the data touched, when, and how it changed.

It shows the path the data actually took. It does not show what a third party did with it, and it covers only what passed through the monitored environment.

The public log

The log is the keystone. It is public, append-only, hash-chained, and readable by any party. Because any party can read it and check the chain, no institution is the sole thing another party must trust.

Once a day, an independent time-stamp issuer stamps the log’s head — a single stamp that pins the entire chain below it to a neutral clock. That is why the time-stamp is not a per-record cost: one stamp per day covers every record that day, and it exists to be the neutral clock for a dispute, not to prove existence (the signature already does that).

# What the daily time-stamp pins log head (end of day 2026-09-22) = sha256 of the whole chain up to that day → one independent stamp on that head → the entire day’s log is pinned to an outside clock if WorkSigned itself ceases to operate, the public log + the stamp still prove the history

Standards

The record is not a proprietary format. The snapshot hash is a standard SHA-256 digest, the signature is a standard public-key signature verifiable with the published key, and the daily time-stamp is issued by an independent time-stamp issuer — so a verifier checks the work against open standards rather than trusting WorkSigned. The signing is open and the logs are append-only and public: any party can confirm exactly what produced a given record, and the private key never leaves the authority. It is also light: only truncated hashes and metadata are stored per record — a few hundred bytes — never the researcher’s files.
