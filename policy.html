Data & security policy

WorkSigned is a signing authority, and its value depends on you trusting it to keep your data yours. This policy states, plainly, what we store, what we don't, and how the highest-security workspaces are encrypted so that we cannot read your files — and so a compromise of our servers can't hand your data and keys to an attacker.

What data we keep

The short version: we do not log user data. The only data we retain about you is what's strictly required to bill your account and let you log in. Your research, your files, your activity, your conversations, and your trail are yours — not a data set we mine, sell, or read.

Rule of thumb: if it isn't needed to run your account (billing) or prove you're you (login), we don't keep a copy of it on the platform as a logged record.

No user-data logging

WorkSigned does not log the content of your work. We do not record your research to a searchable or analyzable store, we do not train on it, and we do not use it to build profiles or products. The trail the platform produces is yours — it belongs to you and to your project, and it is the artifact that is sealed and signed, not a backend log we keep for ourselves.

  • No logging of research content, file contents, or conversation content.
  • No training on your data. No mining, no licensing, no resale.
  • Server logs (for operations) are user-auditable — you can see what they are.

Billing & login data

The only user data we retain is the minimum needed to operate your account:

  • Login. Your identity (email, credentials / auth tokens) so you can sign in.
  • Billing. Account and payment details required to bill you — held under standard PCI-style handling, not on the research data path.

That's it. It is functionally separated from your research data, and it is never combined into an analyzable profile.

Encrypted cloud workspace An optional, maximum-security mode.

For the most sensitive work, you can run an encrypted cloud workspace. In this mode your project files live inside an encrypted volume — a full-disk-encrypted image — and the keys to decrypt it are supplied by you at runtime. The platform mounts and works with the volume while you're logged in, but at rest it is ciphertext we cannot open.

  • Files live in an encrypted image (block-level encryption), not as plaintext on our storage.
  • The decryption key comes from you at runtime — not from us, not from a database row we control.
  • Even an operator of the storage cannot read the files without your key.
This is the difference between "we promise not to read your files" and "we can't read your files." Encrypted-workspace mode gives you the second one.

How your keys are stored

We deliberately do not store your decryption keys in a plain database, and we do not keep them in any form we could use ourselves. Instead, the key material is protected asymmetrically and placed in an unconventional blob — not a readable field in a table, but a sealed object that requires a specific private key to open.

Put concretely:

  • Your key is asymmetrically sealed (encrypted/appended with a public key) into a blob. Only the matching private key can unseal it.
  • The private key is not on any of our online servers. It exists on a dedicated air-gapped machine that is not reachable from the network.
  • At runtime, the key comes from you. We never hold a usable copy of the plaintext key in our systems.
The blob is never read on a live server — only ever appended to. The online system can append sealed fragments to the blob, but it cannot open, decrypt, or even read the contents of what it writes. To the live infrastructure the blob is opaque ciphertext: it stores bytes it has no key to interpret. So there is no server-side read path, no debug endpoint, and no admin action that exposes your key — because the code that touches the blob is, by construction, unable to.

The design goal is that no single point — not our database, not our servers, not an inside operator, not even the live code path that writes the blob — can produce your plaintext key or your plaintext files on its own.

Key recovery

If you lose access to your keys, the sealed blob is not lost — it is recovered through the air-gapped path:

  1. The sealed blob (your key, asymmetrically protected) is transferred to our air-gapped machine.
  2. That machine, holding the private key, unseals the blob and recovers your key.
  3. Access to your workspace is restored.

Because the private key only exists on a machine that is physically offline, recovery is something that can be done deliberately, at the machine — not something an attacker can trigger remotely against our live infrastructure.

This is feasible and is a recognized pattern — it is the same shape as customer-managed-key (BYOK) encryption with a hardware-isolated recovery path, as used by HSM-backed key services and GPG key-recovery schemes. The security property you get: remote compromise of our online systems cannot reach the private key, so it cannot be used to bulk-decrypt your data.

Why it works

The whole design reduces to one property: your data keys and your files cannot be leaked together, because no one we operate holds both.

  • Files are encrypted at rest; the key is supplied by you at runtime.
  • Keys are asymmetrically sealed into a blob — not a plain database, and never read on a live server; only ever appended to.
  • The private key lives only on an air-gapped machine, unreachable from the network.

So a hacker who compromises our servers gets encrypted files and an unsealable blob, but not the key that opens either. A bulk exfiltration of "the data and its keys" is structurally impossible, because the key and the ciphertext are separated by a network boundary that an attacker can cross on only one side at a time.
